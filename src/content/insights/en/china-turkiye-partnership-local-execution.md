A commercial relationship between a Chinese manufacturer and a customer in Türkiye needs a clear answer to a practical question: how will the proposed cooperation work locally? Product capability and senior-level interest provide a starting point. The operating proposition must also explain how requirements will be clarified, decisions coordinated and the relationship supported.

The right model depends on the product, customer and scope of the engagement. Nationality alone is not a reliable guide to how a particular company makes decisions. The useful work is to understand the organisations involved and design the interface between them.

## Translate the customer requirement into a shared brief

The first task is to establish what the customer is evaluating. The brief should distinguish the intended application, required support, proposed delivery scope and questions still under discussion. A broad expression of interest should remain clearly separate from a confirmed commercial commitment.

Where teams work in different languages, important terms should be checked for meaning as well as translated. “Local support,” for example, could refer to a contact person, remote technical access or an on-site service capability. Those are different operating commitments and should be discussed explicitly.

A shared brief creates a basis for the manufacturer, customer and local counterpart to assess the same proposition.

## Choose a partner for the role the work requires

Local counterparties can perform very different functions: introducing customers, coordinating commercial discussions, supporting technical communication or delivering a defined service. A strong relationship in one area does not establish capability in all the others.

Before evaluating candidates, define the role and its boundaries. Then examine the resources, relevant experience and decision-maker access required to perform it. Ask how the candidate would handle the actual workstream, which activities they would own and where they would depend on another party.

This role-based approach also helps distinguish representation from delivery responsibility. The manufacturer and customer should understand exactly what the local party is empowered and equipped to do.

## Make the international–local hand-off explicit

A workable model assigns responsibility to the points at which information or action crosses an organisational boundary. Who clarifies a technical question? Who confirms that the response addresses the customer's request? Who can revise the offer? Who maintains continuity after a meeting?

These responsibilities should connect to named roles on both sides. Relying on a single well-connected individual without an internal response structure can leave progress dependent on personal availability.

For example, in an illustrative equipment discussion, a local coordinator may gather the customer's operating requirements while the manufacturer prepares the technical response. The commercial owner then confirms any implications for the proposal. The customer receives one consistent position rather than separate messages that have not been reconciled.

## Track the opportunity through confirmed milestones

An opportunity review should show what has been agreed, what remains indicative and what evidence is required next. Useful milestones might include confirmation of the application, clarification of the support model or completion of a defined proposal review. The parties should agree which milestones are relevant to their engagement.

The purpose is to prevent activity from being mistaken for advancement. A visit can be productive without changing the commercial status of the opportunity. A short written confirmation of a critical requirement may be more consequential than another broad presentation.

## Build continuity into the relationship

Before expanding the scope of cooperation, leadership should ask:

- Is the proposed local support model understood in the same way by all parties?
- Does each responsibility have an owner with the authority and capacity to act?
- Which assumptions still need direct confirmation from the customer?
- Can the working relationship continue effectively if a key individual is unavailable?

The quality of a cross-border partnership becomes visible in its everyday coordination. A clear local operating model gives international capability a more credible route into a lasting customer relationship.
